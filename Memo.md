# Where should the next two hires actually go?

**To:** Priya Raman, Head of Customer Experience
**From:** Banao (analysis on the ticket categorization work you asked for)

Hi Priya,

Quick heads up before headcount gets locked in — the numbers your team's been looking at aren't quite telling the real story, and it's worth five minutes before Billing gets those two hires.

## What we actually did

You asked for a monthly chart of tickets by category and by team. Simple enough, except we noticed something first: the category tag on every ticket gets set by your chat bot in the first few seconds of a conversation, and agents almost never go back and fix it if it's wrong. So before we could chart anything honestly, we had to ask a more basic question — are the labels even right?

They weren't, a lot of the time. So we had an AI actually read the full ticket — the customer's own words and the agent's closing note — and re-decide what it was really about. Not a new label system, the same 11 categories you already use, just applied by something that actually reads the ticket instead of guessing off the first sentence.

We did this for almost 6,100 tickets, a bit over half your total volume.

## The number that matters

| Team | What your reports say | What's actually happening |
|---|---|---|
| Billing | 23.9% of tickets | **13.9%** |
| Logistics | 16.6% of tickets | **30.4%** |
| Returns Desk | 9.4% of tickets | 18.3% |

Billing looks like your biggest queue almost entirely because customers mention "payment" or "refund" in their opening message even when the real issue is a late or lost delivery — the bot hears "money," tags it Billing, and nobody corrects it before it's counted. Logistics ends up quietly absorbing the actual work, then eating a second hit when the ticket gets handed off to them anyway, hidden from the metrics because your current system only fully tracks hand-offs going forward, not on anything migrated from the old helpdesk.

Here's a real example from your own data, lightly reworded: a customer wrote in asking to cancel an order before it shipped. Got tagged Billing (there was a refund involved), got counted as a Billing ticket in every report you've seen — but the actual work, the actual reason someone had to touch it, was purely a cancellation-and-refund case that never should've been anywhere near Billing's numbers. Multiply that pattern across a few thousand tickets and the picture shifts a lot.

## What this means for the ₹9L

Two hires, roughly ₹9 lakh a year, going to whichever team is genuinely carrying the most tickets. Based on the official numbers, that's Billing. Based on what customers actually wrote and what agents actually did to resolve it, **Logistics is nearly double Billing's real volume** — 30.4% versus 13.9%. Putting both hires on Billing right now would mean staffing up the team that, once you strip out the mislabeling, turns out to be one of your lighter queues.

## How sure are we

We didn't just trust the AI either. Twenty tickets, picked at random, we read ourselves — no shortcuts, actual judgment calls — and checked both the old label and the AI's label against what we personally thought was correct. Old labels matched our read 55% of the time. The AI's corrected labels matched 85% of the time. That's the difference between a coin flip and something you can actually plan a hire around.

One thing we're not going to pretend: this covers January 2025 through November 2025, not all the way to June 2026. We ran out of processing time before reaching the most recent months. The pattern held steady and strong across everything we checked, so we don't expect it to flip in the missing months, but we'd rather tell you that gap exists than quietly paper over it.

## A genuinely hard edge case, in case it comes up

A handful of tickets don't have one clean answer no matter who's labeling them. Someone writes in because one earbud stopped playing audio, agent resolves it by sending a replacement — is that an audio quality issue or a warranty issue? Both are true at once. We didn't force a tiebreaker rule for these; there just isn't a clean one; it comes down to whether you're tracking the symptom or the resolution, and that's a call for your team, not ours.

Happy to walk through any individual ticket, or run the remaining months if it'd help before you finalize the hiring call.
